Chapter 1: The Phone Call at Seven

It was a Wednesday in July in Bengaluru. The rain was falling like it never planned to stop. Ravi was making breakfast in the small kitchen. His wife, Lakshmi, was looking for her car keys. Their daughter, Anu, who was seven, was sitting at the table and drawing a cat with six legs.

At seven o'clock, Ravi's phone rang. It was the school.

"Good morning, Mr. Menon," said a kind voice. "The school is closed today. The road outside is under water. If the rain stops, we will open again tomorrow."

Ravi put down the phone and looked at Lakshmi. Lakshmi looked at Ravi. Anu looked at both of them and smiled a big smile.

"No school?" she asked.

"No school," said Ravi.

"I can't stay home," said Lakshmi. "I have three patients this morning, and one of them is a baby." Lakshmi was a children's doctor at a hospital across the city. "You should call your mother."

Ravi called his mother. She was visiting his sister in Chennai for the week. He called their neighbor, Mrs. Fernandes. She was going to the dentist. He called his friend Suresh, who also had children. Suresh was already at home with four kids, and he sounded tired.

Ravi had a very important meeting at ten o'clock. A big client from Jakarta was coming to see the new design for a mobile app. Ravi and his team had worked on it for two months.

"I'll take her with me," he said.

Lakshmi kissed them both. "Good luck," she said. "You might need it."

Chapter 2: The Rules

In the taxi, Ravi explained the rules.

"Rule number one," he said. "At the office, we use quiet voices. Rule number two: you don't touch anything on anyone's desk. Rule number three is the most important. If I am in a meeting, you stay with Priya and you don't come in. Okay?"

"Who is Priya?" asked Anu.

"Priya works with me. She is very nice. She has a plant on her desk that is older than you."

Anu thought about this. "If I follow all the rules, can I have ice cream after?"

Ravi smiled. Anu was seven, but she was a very good businesswoman. "If you follow all the rules, you will get ice cream after," he said.

They shook hands on it.

Chapter 3: The Office

The office was on the ninth floor of a glass building. When they arrived, people were running around and carrying laptops. Somebody was printing documents. Somebody else was cleaning the big screen in the meeting room. Everyone looked nervous.

"Ravi!" said Priya. "The client's flight landed early. They might be here at half past nine. If they like the design, we'll get the contract." Then she saw Anu and her face changed. "Oh! Hello!"

"Hello," said Anu in a very quiet voice. She was following rule number one.

Ravi explained the situation. Priya laughed. "Don't worry. She can sit with me. I'm only checking emails this morning."

So Anu sat next to Priya's desk with her drawing book and her colored pencils. She looked at the famous plant. It was not very exciting. It was just green.

Ravi went to his desk and opened his laptop. He was practicing his presentation for the tenth time when a small voice said, "Papa?"

He turned around. Anu was standing behind him.

"What is it?"

"The cat in your app," she said, pointing at his screen. "Why is it so sad?"

On the screen, there was a little cartoon cat. It was part of the app. The app helped families save money, and the cat appeared every time someone spent too much. The cat had a long face and big, wet eyes.

"It's supposed to be sad," Ravi said. "If you spend too much money, the cat gets sad, and then you stop spending."

Anu looked at the cat for a long time. "I wouldn't stop," she said. "I would just close the app. Nobody likes a sad cat."

Ravi opened his mouth, and then he closed it again. "Rule number two," he said. "Go back to Priya, please."

Chapter 4: The Meeting

At twenty past nine, the clients arrived. There were three of them. One was a tall woman called Ms. Santoso, and one was a young man with a blue tie. The third was an older man who said very little. Ravi's boss, Mr. Kapoor, took them into the meeting room. Ravi followed with his laptop.

The meeting started well. Ravi showed them the colors, the buttons and the simple menus. Ms. Santoso was nodding. The young man was taking notes. Then Ravi showed them the cat.

Nobody said anything.

"The cat is sad," Ravi explained, "because the user spent too much money."

"Hmm," said Ms. Santoso.

At last the older man said something. "In our tests, people don't like to feel bad," he said. "They might delete the app."

Ravi felt his face get hot. He was thinking about Anu's words. Nobody likes a sad cat. While he was trying to find an answer, he heard something behind the glass door.

It was Anu. She was holding a piece of paper against the glass.

On the paper was a new drawing of the cat. But this cat was not sad. It was smiling, and it was holding a small coin in its paw. Above it, in big, careful letters, were the words "GOOD JOB! YOU SAVED!"

Priya was running toward the door with a terrified face. Mr. Kapoor turned around. Ravi wanted the floor to open under his feet.

But Ms. Santoso was looking at the drawing. Then she laughed. It was a real laugh.

"Who is that?" she asked.

"That," said Ravi slowly, "is my daughter. Her school is closed because of the rain. I'm very sorry. She should be with my colleague."

"No, wait," said Ms. Santoso. "Bring her in. I want to see that drawing."

Chapter 5: The New Cat

Anu came into the meeting room. She was holding her paper with both hands, and she was looking at the floor. For once, she was following rule number one perfectly.

"Can you tell us about your cat?" asked Ms. Santoso.

Anu looked at her father. He nodded.

"The sad cat is not nice," Anu said. "If the cat is sad, people will close the app. But if the cat is happy when you save money, you will want to see it again. So you will save more money." She thought for a moment. "That's what happens with my grandmother. If I finish my homework, she gives me a piece of candy. If I don't finish it, she doesn't shout. She just doesn't give me one."

The older man smiled for the first time that morning.

Ms. Santoso turned to Ravi. "Can your team change the design?" she asked. "A happy cat for good choices, not a sad cat for bad ones."

Ravi looked at Mr. Kapoor. Mr. Kapoor looked like a man who was doing math in his head very quickly.

"We can have a new version next week," said Ravi.

"Good," said Ms. Santoso. She stood up and shook Anu's hand. "Thank you for your help, young lady. If you ever want a job, call me."

"I'm seven," said Anu.

"I can wait," said Ms. Santoso.

Chapter 6: Ice Cream

After the clients went to their hotel, Mr. Kapoor called Ravi into his office. Ravi was expecting a serious talk.

"Next time," said Mr. Kapoor, "you should tell me if your child is coming. Priya almost had a heart attack." Then he smiled. "But good meeting. Very good meeting."

At five o'clock, the rain was still falling, but more gently. Ravi and Anu stopped at a small ice cream shop near their apartment. Anu chose mango. Ravi chose mango too.

"Papa," said Anu, "did I follow all the rules?"

Ravi thought about it carefully. "You followed rule number one," he said. "You broke rule number three. And rule number two, I'm not sure."

Anu looked a little sad.

"But you also helped me," he said. "So I think you should get ice cream. And maybe a piece of candy from your grandmother too."

When they got home, Lakshmi was already in the kitchen. She was making tea and looking very tired after her long day.

"How was the meeting?" she asked.

Ravi and Anu looked at each other.

"You might want to sit down," said Ravi. "It's a long story, and it has a cat in it."
