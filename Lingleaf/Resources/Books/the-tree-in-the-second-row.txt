Part 1

Liza lived in Manila with her family. She worked in an office in the city center. Her son, Nico, was eight, and her daughter, Pia, was five. Her husband, Ramon, drove a taxi at night. So the mornings in their home were always a little crazy.

On Monday evening, Nico came home with a letter from school. "Mom, we're going to do a play on Friday," he said. "And I'm going to be a tree!"

"A tree? That's wonderful," said Liza. She looked at the letter.

The play was on Friday at three o'clock. Every child had to bring a costume. Liza looked at her phone, and she stopped smiling. On Friday at two, she had a big meeting with new clients.

"You're going to come, right?" asked Nico.

Liza looked at his face. "I'll be there," she said. "I promise."

The next morning, she talked to her manager, Mr. Tan. "I have to leave early on Friday," she said. "Can we move the meeting to the morning?"

Mr. Tan looked at his screen for a long time. "The clients are flying in from Singapore," he said. "Their plane arrives at noon. I'm sorry, Liza. We can't change it."

Liza went back to her desk. She was very unhappy. Her colleague, Maricel, looked at her.

"What's wrong?" asked Maricel.

Liza told her everything.

"I know the project too," said Maricel. "You can start the meeting. Then I'll finish it for you. You can go at two thirty."

"Are you sure?" asked Liza.

"Of course. You helped me last month, when my mother was sick."

"Thank you, Maricel. I won't forget this."

Part 2

But there was still the costume. On Wednesday, Liza came home at eight. She was tired, and she had to cook dinner.

Pia wanted a story. Nico wanted to practice his line for the play. He only had one line: "I am the oldest tree." He said it about a hundred times.

At ten o'clock, the children were finally asleep. Liza sat at the kitchen table with paper, glue and scissors. She didn't know what to do.

Then her mother called. Grandma Carmen lived near them, and she always went to bed late.

"Why are you still awake?" she asked.

"I have to make a tree," said Liza.

Her mother laughed. "I made costumes for you and your brothers for years. I'm going to come over tomorrow evening. Buy more green paper."

On Thursday evening, the two women worked together at the table. Liza cut out the green leaves. Grandma Carmen glued them onto an old brown T-shirt. Nico tried it on. He turned around slowly.

"I look like a real tree!" he said.

"A real tree doesn't talk this much," said Grandma Carmen.

On Friday, the meeting started at two. Liza spoke first, and her hands were shaking a little. The clients asked a lot of questions. At two thirty, Maricel stood up and continued the presentation. Liza smiled at her and quietly walked out.

The traffic was terrible. Liza took a motorbike taxi, because the cars were not moving. She arrived at the school at three fifteen. The hall was dark and full of parents. She sat down at the back.

On the stage, there were six trees in two rows. Nico was in the second row. He was the shortest tree, but his leaves were the biggest.

Then it was his turn. "I am the oldest tree," he said in a big voice. Then he looked for his mother. Liza waved. Nico waved back with a branch. Two of the leaves fell on the floor.

Everybody laughed, and Nico laughed too.

After the play, he ran to her. "You came!" he said.

"I promised," said Liza. "And I try to keep my promises."

That evening, she sent a message to Maricel: "Thank you. Next time, I'll help you." Then she sat on the sofa with Nico and Pia. Nico was still wearing his costume. He wanted to sleep in it.

"Not tonight," said Liza. "Trees have to brush their teeth too."
