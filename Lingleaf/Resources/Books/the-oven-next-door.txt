Part 1

Emre started his new job on a Monday. He was a cook in a small restaurant in Istanbul. The restaurant was called The Blue Table. It had only twelve tables, but it was always full.

His boss was a woman called Selin. She was the head cook, and she was very fast. On his first day, she gave him a white jacket. Then she gave him a list of rules.

"You have to be here at two o'clock every day," she said. "You have to wash your hands often. And you have to taste everything before it goes to a customer."

Emre was nervous, but he learned quickly. He cut onions and peppers for hours. He washed big pots. He made the rice and the salads. By Thursday, he knew the kitchen well.

Part 2

On Friday morning, Selin called him. "Emre, I can't come today," she said. "My son is sick. I have to take him to the doctor."

"I'm sorry," said Emre. "I hope he feels better soon."

"Thank you. But we have a big group tonight. Thirty people from an office are coming at seven. They want the fish menu. Can you do it?"

For a moment, Emre didn't say anything. Thirty people was a lot for a new cook. "I'll try," he said at last. "I promise I'll do my best."

"Good. Gül will help you. Call me if you have a problem."

Gül was the waiter. She was young and very calm. She knew every customer by name.

Emre arrived at the restaurant at noon, two hours early. He made a plan on a piece of paper. He needed thirty plates of fish, thirty salads and rice for everyone. The dessert was easy, because it was already in the fridge.

At four o'clock, he turned on the big oven. Nothing happened. He turned it off and on again. Still nothing. The oven was dead.

Emre called Selin. "The oven isn't working," he said. "What do I do?"

"Oh no," said Selin. "The repairman can't come before Monday. You have to find another oven."

Emre ran out into the street. Next door, there was a small bakery. The baker was an old man called Hasan. He was cleaning his big bread oven. His workday finished at three, but the oven was still hot.

"Excuse me," said Emre. "I have a big problem." He explained everything quickly.

Hasan listened and then smiled. "My oven is free until tomorrow morning," he said. "Bring your fish. I'll help you."

For two hours, Emre and Gül carried trays between kitchens. Emre cooked the fish in the bakery oven. It was hotter than his oven, so he watched it carefully. Hasan helped him. He knew his old oven very well.

At seven o'clock, the group arrived. They were loud and hungry. Gül brought the salads and the bread. Then Emre and Hasan carried the fish from the bakery. People in the street stopped and looked. The smell was wonderful.

The customers ate everything. At the end, one woman came into the kitchen. "Who cooked the fish?" she asked. "It was the best fish of my life."

Emre was surprised, and his face went red. "Two people cooked it," he said. "Me and my neighbor."

At ten, Emre went to the bakery. "Thank you, Hasan," he said. "I'll bring you a plate tomorrow, I promise."

At eleven, Emre cleaned the kitchen. He was very tired, but he was happy. Then his phone rang. It was Selin.

"Gül told me everything," she said. "You did a great job. How did you think of the bakery?"

"I didn't think," said Emre. "I just ran."

Selin laughed. "That's the first rule of a kitchen. When there's a problem, you have to move fast."

The next morning, Emre took a plate of fish to the bakery. Hasan gave him a bag of warm bread. "Come back when your oven breaks again," he said.
