Chapter 1: A City of Stairs

Joaquín is fifteen, and he lives in Valparaíso, Chile. It is a city that climbs up steep hills above the ocean. His street is so steep that the houses look like they are standing on each other's shoulders. Every morning he walks down two hundred and six stairs to get to school. He has counted them many times.

Joaquín used to play the drums. When he was twelve, his uncle gave him an old drum kit, which filled half of his bedroom. He used to practice every afternoon after school. The neighbors used to bang on the wall. In the end, his mother sold the drums to a boy who lived near the port.

Now Joaquín makes beats on his phone. He uses a free app that lets him record sounds and turn them into music. He has recorded the bells of the funicular, the old train that carries people up the hill. He has recorded the shouts of the man who sells bread from a basket. He has even recorded the sea lions that sleep near the fish market.

He has never shown his music to anyone. Not even to Daniela, who has been his best friend since they were six.

Chapter 2: The Song That Escaped

One Tuesday, Joaquín and Daniela were sitting on the school wall at lunch. Daniela was drawing in her notebook, as she always did. Joaquín had his headphones on, and his foot was moving to a beat.

"What are you listening to?" Daniela asked.

"Nothing," he said. "Just a song."

She took one side of his headphones and put it to her ear. Her eyes opened wide. "Who made this?" she asked. "It sounds like the city. I can hear the funicular!"

Joaquín's face went hot. "I made it," he said quietly. "It's just a thing I do."

"Joaquín, this is really good. Can you send it to me?"

He didn't want to say no, so he sent it. That evening, Daniela made a short video for her account. It showed her drawings of the colorful houses on the hills, and Joaquín's beat played behind them. She posted it before dinner.

By the next morning, the video had more than forty thousand views.

Chapter 3: Forty Thousand Strangers

He found out in the worst way. A boy who sat behind him in math class leaned forward. "Hey, have you seen Daniela's video?" he said. "The music is amazing. Who's the artist?"

Joaquín opened the app under his desk. Thousands of people had written comments. Many of them asked the same question: "What is this song?" Under the video, Daniela had written: "Music by a shy friend. He won't tell you his name."

He felt sick and proud at the same time. Strangers in Mexico, Spain and Japan liked something that he had made in his bedroom. But nobody had asked him if he wanted this.

At lunch he didn't go to the wall. He went to the library instead, which was the quietest place in the school. Daniela found him there ten minutes later.

"Are you angry?" she asked. "I thought you would be happy."

"You didn't ask me," he said. "That song was mine. Now everybody has heard it, and I wasn't ready."

Daniela sat down. For a long time, she said nothing. Then she said, "You're right. I should have asked. I'm sorry." She took out her phone. "I can delete it right now if you want."

Joaquín looked at the comments again. A girl in Brazil wrote, "This song makes me miss a city I have never visited."

"Wait," he said. "Don't delete it yet. Let me think."

Chapter 4: The Message

That night, Joaquín couldn't sleep. He lay on his bed and listened to the ships in the port. His phone made a sound. It was a message from an account that he didn't know.

"Hi. My name is Isabel. I'm a student who runs the radio station at the university here in Valparaíso. Daniela gave me your name, and I hope that's okay. We have a show on Saturdays for young local artists. Would you like to play your music live?"

Joaquín sat up. Playing live meant real people, in a real room, watching him. "I have never done anything like this," he thought. His hands started to shake just from thinking about it.

He wrote back, "I'm not an artist. I just make beats on my phone."

Isabel answered quickly. "Everyone who has played on our show started somewhere. Some of them started on their phones, too. Think about it. You have until Thursday."

He turned off his phone and looked at the ceiling. He thought about the drums that his mother sold. He used to love the moment before he started playing, when everything was quiet. Now he missed that feeling.

Chapter 5: Daniela's Idea

On Wednesday, he told Daniela about the message.

"I know," she said. "I'm the one who told Isabel. But I asked her to write to you directly this time. I didn't want to decide for you again."

Joaquín laughed for the first time that week. "You're learning," he said.

"So? Are you going to do it?"

"I don't know. What if I make a mistake? What if nobody likes it? What if my phone dies in the middle of the song?"

Daniela thought for a moment. "What if you don't do it alone?" she asked. "I could draw while you play. Playing live, on a big screen, like in the video. People can watch my drawing, and you can hide behind it a little."

It was a strange idea, but it was also a good one. Joaquín imagined the room. He imagined people looking at the screen, not at him. His heart slowed down a little.

On Thursday morning, he wrote to Isabel. "Okay. But my friend comes too."

Chapter 6: Saturday

The radio station was in an old building near the top of a hill. Its windows looked over the whole bay, which was blue and silver in the evening light. About thirty people were sitting on the floor and on old sofas. Some of them were holding phones, ready to record.

Near the door, Joaquín noticed a familiar face. It was his mother. He didn't tell her about the show, so Daniela probably did. His mother smiled and gave him a small wave.

Isabel was a tall girl with green glasses who talked very fast. "You're on after the poet," she said. "Fifteen minutes. Don't worry. Nobody here bites."

Joaquín connected his phone to the speakers. His fingers were cold. Daniela set up her tablet, and her first line appeared on the big screen behind them.

"Ready?" she whispered.

"No," he said. "Let's go."

He pressed play. The first sound was the bell of the funicular. Then the bread seller's voice came in, then the sea lions, and then the beat. Behind him, Daniela began to draw a staircase that climbed up the screen.

For the first minute, Joaquín looked only at his phone. Then he noticed something. A woman in the front row was moving her head to the music. A boy near the window was smiling. An old man who was standing at the back closed his eyes.

"I have never seen people listen to my music before," he thought. It felt different from forty thousand views. It felt real.

Chapter 7: The Sound of the Stairs

When the song ended, there was a short silence. Then everybody clapped. Some people even shouted. Joaquín didn't know what to do with his hands, so he waved. Daniela bowed, and everyone laughed.

After the show, the old man came to talk to him. "That bell," he said. "I have taken that funicular every day for fifty years. I didn't know it could be music."

Isabel gave him a high five. "Come back next month," she said. "Bring a new song."

His mother was waiting for him by the door. For a moment, she didn't say anything. Then she hugged him.

"I'm sorry about the drums," she said. "I didn't know how much you missed them."

"It's okay," said Joaquín. "I have found other drums. The whole city is a drum."

Walking home, Joaquín and Daniela climbed the two hundred and six stairs together. Halfway up, he stopped and took out his phone.

"What are you doing?" Daniela asked.

"Recording," he said. He held the phone near the ground. "Walk up the stairs. Slowly."

She walked, and her shoes made a soft sound on each step. Joaquín listened to it carefully. It was a good rhythm. It was the start of something new.

"Next time," he said, "you can put my name on the video."
