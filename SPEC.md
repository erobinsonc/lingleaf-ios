# Lingleaf — product spec

Shared by [lingleaf-ios](https://github.com/erobinsonc/lingleaf-ios) and
[lingleaf-android](https://github.com/erobinsonc/lingleaf-android). Both
apps implement every scenario below; each scenario is covered by tests with
the same intent on both platforms. A change to behaviour updates this file in
**both** repositories.

## Principles

- Learn English by reading real books. The reader is the product.
- No accounts, ads or tracking. Data stays on the device.
- Network only for: Gutenberg search (`gutendex.com`), book downloads and covers
  (`www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/`) and word definitions (`api.dictionaryapi.dev`). HTTPS only,
  to these hosts only: a redirect is followed only to them (at most 5); no cookies, no disk cache.
  Outside data is untrusted even from these hosts: dictionary fields are cut to 500 characters
  (definition) and 100 (word, IPA, part of speech); JSON nested deeper than 64 levels is refused;
  a search shows each book once, at most 32, and a recommended book always as shipped; titles and
  authors lose invisible control, formatting and blank-filler characters, keep at most 4 accents per
  letter and are at most 200 characters.
  Paragraphs over 2,000 characters are cut at the last sentence end that fits (else at a space);
  a word's saved sentence is at most 600 characters around the word. Files are read and written
  only under the app's own plain names. Saved data read back (also from a restored backup) is brought
  into range: the daily goal 5–60 minutes, the text size 14–34, the speech rate within its slider, reading
  progress inside the book, a date that can't be read becomes due now; a streak counts at most 3,650 days.
- Translation runs on the device (Apple Translation on iOS, Google ML Kit on Android). On Android, ML Kit
  starts (and downloads nothing) only after the learner's first word tap, and the card says once
  "Translation uses Google's on-device model; Google receives diagnostics."
- Legal notices: links are fixed constants built by the app and open in the system browser, except the privacy policy, which iOS
  opens in the in-app browser (Safari View Controller) and Android in a Custom Tab, at the Spanish summary
  (`#política-de-privacidad-de-lingleaf-resumen-en-español`) when the app is in Spanish. Settings → About
  links the privacy policy (`https://github.com/erobinsonc/lingleaf-ios/blob/main/PRIVACY.md`, the same file
  for both apps). Search results say "Project Gutenberg books are public domain in the US. Elsewhere,
  check your country's laws." Definitions credit Wiktionary (CC BY-SA). The app is for ages 13 and up; there is
  no age gate.
- Interface in English and Spanish; more languages welcome.

| S3 | Tap a word | Any word of the text can be tapped, in any script (a word is a run of letters with their accents, Shared logic: names and loanwords such as "Joaquín", "café", "Kraków" are one word); the English help is for English words (the dictionary for Latin-script words only). Source: Wiktionary." (with the part of speech first when there is one). A past or participle of the irregular-verb table that is not also another word ("tore", "torn", "sprang", "lit", "lain") is always that verb, except as a name: its meaning is the table's Spanish meanings (else the translation of "to {base}"), its definition is a verb's; "lied" and "hanged" are the regular twin ("mentir", "ahorcar"). An -s or -ing form of the table ("lights", "bears", "springs", "meeting") is that verb only where its sentence proves it (Shared logic); elsewhere it is an ordinary word: the dictionary and the translation of the word as tapped, no table meaning ("the lights", "three bears", "a meeting"). "lying", "lies", "hanging", "hangs" take their sense from the next word ("lying to" → "mentir", "lying on" → "estar acostado"); when it doesn't tell, the card is unsure with the two senses: "to lie — estar acostado, tumbarse" / "to lie — mentir". "lies" no step proves a verb ("She tells lies") is unsure between the twin's noun and the verb: "the lie — mentira" / "to lie — estar, encontrarse" (the table's `nounEs` and `sEs`); after a determiner it is that noun ("his lies" → "lie"). An -s form of a base that is mostly a noun (`nounFirst`) after a noun subject with no object after it ("The bell rings") is unsure: "to ring — sonar" / "the ring — anillo" (the noun's regular lemma, spelled like the base; chosen, it is saved as "ring" with the label "the ring"). "bound for" and "bound to" + a verb are one expression: the card shows "bound for" / "bound to" with the table's meaning ("rumbo a, con destino a"; "seguro que · obligado a"; outside Spanish the translation of its `query`, "heading for", "certain to"), the dictionary's adjective definition of "bound" (nothing, not "No definition available.", when it has none), and Save stores the expression as one entry (like a verb group, S16). A form that is also another word is read from its sentence: "she left" → "leave", "turn left" → "left", "a bit" → "bit"; when the sentence doesn't tell (unsure), the card doesn't guess: the tapped word (no IPA, translation or definition), the sentence and its translation with "Read from here", a guide line and its two readings as a single choice (the guide says "The sentence's translation tells you which one:" while the sentence's translation shows or is on its way, else "Which one is it in this sentence?" — offline, translation failed or no translation language yet; with no language yet, the language choice comes right below the word, where the translation goes), most common first (`nounFirst`: the noun), none chosen: a verb as "to {base}", a noun as "the {noun}", each with its meaning ("to leave — irse, salir", "the left — izquierda"; "lay" after I/you/we/they or a plural noun subject: "to lie", "to lay"; a form with no noun: its two verbs). Offline, the Spanish meaning of a noun that is a homograph comes from the table too (`nounEs` of the homograph: "left" → "izquierda", "ground" → "suelo, tierra"; the forms of one noun agree); a noun the table has none for (its sense depends on its sound: lead, tear; or it is rare: fell, lay) leaves the rows without a meaning, and so does any row without its meaning while the translation is unavailable, rather than pointing at the one that has it. A verb reading's meaning is the table's first Spanish meaning in Spanish, else the translation of "to {base}", or of the table's English `query` for that sense when it has one ("to lie down" / "to tell a lie", "to hang up" / "to execute by hanging"); if the two rows' meanings still come out the same, each adds the sense's short English `gloss` ("recline", "say what isn't true"); a noun's, the translation of "the {noun}"; dictionary entry and definition match the reading's part of speech: when the table gives the base's IPA, the entry whose pronunciation matches it ("wind" /waɪnd/, not /wɪnd/), its first meaning of that part of speech; when no entry matches the table's IPA, no definition and the table's IPA. A noun of tear, lead or wind takes its pronunciation from its sentence (`nounIpa`, its rules in order, those on the next word before those on the word before: "tears in her eyes", "a tear rolled" → /tɪr/; "a tear in my shirt" → /tɛr/; "made of lead", "lead pipes" → /lɛd/; "took the lead", "the role of lead singer" → /liːd/; wind → /wɪnd/; with no clue, "tears" → /tɪr/ and "leads" → /liːd/, their usual sense) and the entry with that pronunciation; when the sentence doesn't tell ("the tear was small"), no IPA or definition from the dictionary. Words around it that point to two sounds prove neither (`near` words of two sounds: "The detective found lead in the metal pipes" has no IPA); the word right after a lead decides before "with" ("with lead detective", also investigator, engineer, developer… → /liːd/), and "a tear in her voice" is /tɪr/. A noun is spoken as written, except a noun whose sentence proves a sound the voice wouldn't make: lead /lɛd/ is said "led", tear /tɛr/ "tare" (the plurals "leds", "tares"; the IPA shown is the headword's, /lɛd/, /tɛr/). The regular twin ("lied", "mentir") shows the base's IPA from the table (lie /laɪ/, hang /hæŋ/) and no definition; with no entry of that part of speech for the base, the word as tapped, else no definition (never another part of speech's); a reading nothing proves has no definition. Choosing one checks it (the other stays, to change it), shows its IPA and definition (aligned with the rows; "No definition available." only once its look-up has answered, never while it runs), and Listen and Save follow it. On iOS the card opens at the height of its content for a word whose sentence doesn't tell (it can be dragged to full height) and at the usual half height otherwise; at accessibility text sizes it opens at full height. Translation starts when the card opens, alongside the definition. The translation is in the learner's language; a small language button under it changes the language in place. |

| S19 | Story senses | Each of the 18 original stories (S1) ships with a senses file (`<id>.senses.json`, schema version 1) that says how a word, or a group of words, is used at one place in the story: its base form, part of speech, IPA, its Spanish meaning in that sentence and, for a group, the group and its meaning ("his face went red": "se puso colorado"; "free" in "my oven is free": "libre, disponible", not "gratis"). When the tapped word of such a story has an entry, the card shows that sense before the verb-or-noun rule (S3) and the helper verbs (S16): the base form as headword ("In the text: went" when it differs), the sense's IPA between slashes (none when the file has none; never the dictionary's), and a small label (caption size, secondary colour): "Proper name" for a name that is also a word ("The Blue Table", "Mr. Long", "Cape Town"), "Word from another language" ("Bunica", "Amma", "san"), else "US English", "UK English" or "Local English" when the word or meaning is used only there. The meaning replaces the word's own translation: in Spanish the file's meaning; in another language the translation of its source (the source is one exact table (`StorySenseHit.translationSource`): a name or a word from another language: none; a group of words (kind chunk): the group's text, else the base form (the `en` gloss is ignored); a bare word (kind word): its `en` gloss, else its group's text, else none when the story has another sense of that base form or its Spanish meaning contains `no «` (or `no "`), else the base form. With no source the card shows no meaning for the word and the translated sentence tells); a name and a word from another language have none in another language, and a name's Spanish remark ("nombre de la empresa…") shows under the headword. A group is underlined in the card's sentence and shown below the meaning on one line, with its Spanish meaning ("his face went red — se puso colorado"; the group alone in another language). The definition shows only when the sense's kind is word or chunk, its base form is one word and its part of speech is noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, interjection or modal (a modal looks up "verb"; phrasal verb, phrase, proper noun and suffix have none), and the dictionary has an entry of that part of speech; otherwise none: never the dictionary's first meaning of another part of speech. A sense's Spanish note shows under it, and a name's Spanish remark, in Spanish only; a name shows no language chip (it has no meaning). A name and a word from another language are not offered to save: the card has no "Save word" or "I know it", because their spelling is also a common word ("Long" in "Mr. Long"). The IPA line is hidden from screen readers (Listen says the word); the headword is read as English and the meaning in the card's language; the group line is read "Group: …". "Save word" and "I know it" store the base form (a group's: "go red") with the sense (S4's `sense` is "s:" and the sense's key in the story's file, e.g. "s:get_fetch"), the story's id, the sense's meaning and IPA, the sentence, the tapped form and, for a group, where the group is in the sentence. A word is saved once per sense (the same base form and sense in two stories is one entry; `story` is the last one saved): "get" saved from two senses is two entries, which Words and Review name like S4 does, with the first meaning: the label "get (ponerse)" is added only when another entry with a sense of that spelling is saved, and its meaning is the first part of the saved translation (cut at " · "), cut at the first "," or "(" and trimmed; with only one sense saved Words and Review show the plain "get" (unlike S4, whose labels "to wind"/"the wind" come from the reading, not from the meaning). Review's prompt names it plain and shows the sentence when two or more senses of that spelling are saved. Words and Review mark the whole group in the sentence ("his face went red"). In the reader, a word being learned (not one marked "I know it") that was saved from a story sense in that story, with that base form, is underlined only at the places of the story with the same sense, a group as a whole; at a place of the same base form with another sense only that place's own range is left out of the generic underline; everywhere else (a word with no entry, another book) it is underlined as any saved word (S4). The saved sense, story and group are read back within bounds: a sense key outside a–z, 0–9 and `_` (40 at most), a story id that is not a–z/0–9 words joined by single dashes (60 at most), or a group that is not inside the sentence (it is dropped too when the sentence was cut or cleaned) or does not hold the tapped form is dropped; the story, the source and the group are kept only for a word with a story sense, no verb group and no unsure readings (a group is kept when there is no tapped-form range); a `#` in a word is removed.
  A word saved from a story sense also stores its translation source (the table in S19; nothing when the table gives none). Words are translated into another language only from that source, never from the bare spelling; with none, nothing is translated, the old meaning of another language is cleared when the language changes, and Review says "No single meaning out of context. See the sentence." ("Sin significado suelto fuera de contexto. Mira la frase.") instead of promising a translation, and keeps the translated sentence (S7). A plain word saved again as a story sense loses its old meaning and IPA, which are filled in again from the sense. A word without an entry behaves as before (S3, S4, S16). Entries apply only to the library's copy of a bundled story whose paragraphs are exactly those of the bundled text: a book imported or downloaded with the same title, or with that title and author but other text, has none, and a file the app refuses (Shared logic) leaves the story as if it had none. The tapped word of a group may be any of its words that has an entry. |

## Shared logic (same rules, same tests on both platforms)

- Paragraphs: split on blank lines, hard-wrapped lines joined.
- Words (what can be tapped, marked and saved): the pattern (ICU / Java syntax, possessive quantifiers)
  `(?:\p{L}\p{M}{0,4}+)++(?:['’](?:\p{L}\p{M}{0,4}+)++)*+`, applied to the text as it is, left to right, longest
  match. In words: a run of letters of any script, each with at most four combining marks after it ({0,4} counts code
  points, not UTF-16 units; `\p{M}` is Mn, Mc and Me), with an apostrophe (' or ’) between two letters kept inside
  ("don't", "l’homme", "O'Brien", "dogs'" is "dogs"). A word starts with a letter, so marks alone, digits, emoji,
  hyphens and punctuation are not words and end one ("Jean-Luc" is two words, "naïve2" is "naïve"); a letter with a
  fifth mark ends the word and the extra marks belong to no word. ZWNJ (U+200C) and ZWJ (U+200D) are format
  characters, neither letters nor marks: they end a word too (Persian "می‌خواهم" is two words). Composed ("é") and
  decomposed ("e" + U+0301) spellings are each one word, over different numbers of UTF-16 units. Scripts that write no
  spaces (Han, kana, Thai) give one word per run of letters. Offsets are UTF-16 units of the unchanged text. Reading
  and cutting text take time linear in its length, whatever it is made of (a long run of marks, letters or
  apostrophes is read in one pass).
- Word keys (saved words, undo, translations, marking, dictionary): the word with ’ → ', lower-cased with the Unicode
  full lower-case mapping of each character on its own (no locale, no final-sigma rule: "ΟΔΟΣ" → "οδοσ"; "İ" → "i" +
  U+0307), then composed (NFC) when it has a letter outside ASCII, then cut to 600 UTF-16 units (see Cuts). One
  function makes the key, and every id, look-up and match uses it, so a run of letters longer than 600 units (a Han
  run) is saved, found, translated, undone and marked under the same key. "Capitalised" (a name, a sentence start)
  means its first code point has the Unicode Uppercase property (not a titlecase letter such as "ǅ") and the rest of
  the word has none, unless the word is that one code point or has an apostrophe ("O'Brien", "D'Artagnan"); a name (capitalised in mid-sentence) also has some
  lower-case letter, so "I", "A" and "NASA" are not names; "ǅ" is not capitalised.
- English rules work on ASCII words only: base forms (plural, past and -ing endings), the irregular-verb table and the
  verb-or-noun rule never apply to a word with a letter outside ASCII; such a word is its own only key, and next to a
  verb form it counts as an ordinary word or, capitalised in mid-sentence or opening a sentence, as a name (a subject:
  "Joaquín left" reads like "Tom left").
- Dictionary look-up: the key is sent only when it matches `^\p{Latin}(?:\p{Latin}|\p{M}|['’])*\z` (Java:
  `\p{IsLatin}`; `\z`, so a trailing line break does not match) and is at most 50 UTF-16 units (measured on the key,
  after lower-casing and NFC): a Latin-script word with or without accents or apostrophes ("café", "naïve", "résumé",
  a name such as "Joaquín", which just has no entry). Another script (Cyrillic, Greek, Han, Arabic…), a mixed-script
  word or a longer one is never sent and shows "No definition available." with its translation. A reading's base is a
  key too (lower case, NFC) before it is looked up.
- Text in an address (a dictionary word, a catalog search): UTF-8, each byte outside A–Z a–z 0–9 written %XX with
  upper-case hexadecimal digits (space is %20, an apostrophe %27, "é" %C3%A9); nothing else stays as it is. A lone
  surrogate in the text is U+FFFD first (%EF%BF%BD). A catalog
  search is cut to 200 UTF-16 units first: "Les Misérables 東京" is `search=Les%20Mis%C3%A9rables%20%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC`.
- Invisible formatting: text imported or downloaded for a book, and a word's saved sentence, lose their format
  characters (general category Cf: bidi overrides, isolates and marks (LRM U+200E, RLM U+200F, ALM U+061C),
  zero-width spaces, the byte-order mark, soft hyphens, tag characters U+E0001 and U+E0020–E007F, and also the drawn
  Arabic signs of Cf: U+0600–0605, U+06DD, U+070F, U+0890–0891, U+08E2) except ZWNJ and ZWJ, and before any cut to a length (strip, then cut at 600). A book stored before this rule is stripped when it is
  opened: if that changes its text, the text is stored again once, and, only when that is confirmed, the book's paragraph
  count is that of the new text and the place in the book moves back by the number of paragraphs before it that were
  made only of such characters (a place on one of them goes to the next paragraph, a place past the end to the last,
  none left to 0); a book whose text cannot be read is left exactly as it is, and shows nothing. Cf is that of the platform's
  Unicode tables (Swift's standard library on iOS, the Android API level's on Android): a character assigned in one
  version only may be stripped on one platform and not the other, a residual.
- Cuts: a cut at the paragraph (2,000), sentence (600) or any other cap moves back up to 6 UTF-16 units, never to less
  than one unit after the start of the piece, while the unit at the cut continues what comes before it: the second
  half of a surrogate pair; a combining mark (Mn, Mc, Me, in the BMP); a variation selector U+FE00–FE0F; a joiner
  U+200D, or the unit right after one. Residual: a combining mark outside the BMP (e.g. U+1D165) is not recognised and
  may be cut from its letter.

- Base forms (see `TextTools`): the irregular-verb table and a few irregular nouns and comparatives (children,

- Verb or noun (see `IrregularVerbs`), for a homograph of the table (left, saw, felt, found, rose, fell, ground,
  wound, spoke, bit, shot, dove, thought, stole, slew, spat, spelt, smelt, lay, bore, bound, lives, leaves, lead,
  leads, tear, tears, wind, winds), from the words next to it (only spaces between) in its sentence; "up to 2
  adverbs" means listed ones or lower-case words in -ly. The first step that applies decides:
  1. name: capitalised mid-sentence ("Aunt Rose"; not all capitals) or part of a hyphenated word ("left-handed")
     → the word itself, not a verb.
  2. fixed: a listed noun phrase ("the lost and found") → the word itself, not a verb, unless a subject, a form
     of have or be, or an adverb comes right before the phrase or an object word right after it ("she lost and
     found her keys"); a phrase listed for the form ("turn left", "left hand", "right and left", "rose petals", "felt hat",
     "one fell swoop") → its noun; an expression of the form (`expressions`: "bound for", "bound to" + a base verb) →
     that expression, not a verb.
  3. noun: right after a determiner (a, an, the, my, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose, every, no), or after
     one listed adjective that follows one ("a little bit", "the red rose") → its noun. A possessive ("Tom's")
     counts as a determiner except before a form that can be a participle ("Tom's left" may be "Tom has left").
     An -s form also after a quantifier or number (these, many, nine…) or a preposition (of, in, with, to…), and
     with more listed adjectives ("dead leaves").
  3b. opening participle: left, felt, found, ground, wound, shot, thought, spat, spelt, smelt, bound opening the
     sentence, before a listed word (alone, behind, guilty, dead, in, on, by, with…, or a word in -ly) and with a
     comma later in the sentence → the verb ("Left alone, she cried"; not "Felt hats, …", "Thought, like speech, …";
     "Bound for London, …" is step 2).
  4. base: after to, a modal or do (up to 2 adverbs between) → the form as a verb of its own ("to saw", "will
     lead", "to fell"; "to ground" is not); lay, lead, tear, wind opening the sentence (or after "Please") before
     an object word → that verb ("Lead the way").
  5. participle: left, felt, found, ground, wound, shot, thought, spat, spelt, smelt, bound after a form of
     have, be or get (up to 2 adverbs between) → the verb ("had left", "was found").
  6. subject (up to 2 adverbs between): after I, you, he, she, it, we, they, who, which, someone, nobody,
     something…; "one" only after no/every/not; "this"/"that" only before an -s form; "there"/"here" before lay,
     rose, fell; or after a capitalised name (when it opens the sentence: not a listed function word or adjective,
     not in -ly, not before an -s form) → the verb. lives and leaves after a capitalised word opening the sentence
     that is probably a name (not a listed function word, adjective, month, direction or plant name, not in -ly,
     -ful, -less, -ous, not a verb form in -ed/-ing), before a listed preposition or adverb (not "of") or a word in
     -ly, and with no finite verb later in their clause (is, are, had, can… or a listed past such as "shone"; at most
     the next 60 words are looked at) →
     the verb ("Tom lives in…"; not "Double lives in fiction are common").
  7. subjectNoun: left, saw, felt, found, rose, fell, spoke, stole, slew, spat, spelt, smelt, lay, bore, bound
     after a determiner (+ one listed adjective) + a noun, when that determiner opens the sentence or a clause
     (after a comma, a semicolon, and, but, when, that, until…) → the verb ("The sun rose"; not "made of the
     finest felt", "wore a fur stole").
     bit only before an object word or into, off, through, down, hard, deep, at ("The dog bit him"; not "The
     funny bit was…").
  7b. noun + -s form: lives, leaves after a determiner (+ one listed adjective) + a singular noun, the determiner
     opening the sentence or a clause, before a listed preposition or adverb (not "of") or a word in -ly, with no
     finite verb later in their clause → the verb ("The old man lives alone", "The bus leaves at six"; not "the
     willow leaves on the water": listed plant words before leaves are step 2).
  7c. quantifier: left, saw, felt, found, rose, fell, ground, wound, spoke, bit, shot, dove, thought, stole, slew,
     spat, spelt, lay after a quantifier or number (many, few, several, both, two…; also "all the", "both my",
     "some of the", "hundreds of") + (one listed adjective) + a plural noun (-s, or men, people, children…), the
     quantifier opening the sentence or a clause → the verb ("Many people left", "Both girls saw it"); "lay" as
     after "they". Not bore, bound, smelt: a present-tense twin fits a plural subject ("Long speeches bore me").
  8. conjunction: the past forms except ground after and, but, then (up to 2 adverbs between) → the verb.
  9. Else unsure: no irregular link, the word itself (only when its two bases are one: tears → tear).

  I/you/we/they with neither an object word nor a word like down, on, still, awake after it, it is unsure; after
  there/here it is "lie". Without a sentence every homograph is unsure. The word lists are in the table
  (`homographs`, `context`). The card's base form and dictionary look-up and the yellow underline use the same result (an unsure
  occurrence marks no word saved with a sense):
  a verb looks its base up directly; a noun looks its own base up first, then the word as tapped, without following "past tense of …" in its
  definition; unsure looks the word up as before. Listen reads a form taken as a verb (or the regular twin) with the table's spoken form

A JSON null on an optional key (`variety`, `note`, `en`, `chunk`) reads as absent (`ipa` is required, but its value may be null); a repeated key is not detected: the last value wins, and the bundled files have none (a test checks). A group's text and an entry's text also go through the same text rules. "Unassigned" depends on the platform's Unicode version: the shipped files use only assigned scalars. A file is used only for a book whose paragraphs equal those of the bundled text.
  The tests load every bundled file and check every entry against the bundled text.
- Imported .txt: title is the file name without its extension (empty → "Book"); Gutenberg header/footer removed; files over 10 MB or with no text are refused.

## Grammar tab (S20–S25)

The fifth tab, between Words and Settings: "Grammar" / "Gramática", a lightbulb icon. Rules, examples and practice
are bundled in the app (`grammar-rules.json`, below): no download, no new host, no account. The Grammar tab ships
together with its Review items (S25): it is not offered as a way to remember rules before that. Grammar rules data (shared logic, same file and tests on both platforms):

- `grammar-rules.json` (identical in both repos, bundled in the core module; a `.gitattributes` rule keeps JSON files byte for byte, no line-ending conversion, on both platforms) is the only source of Grammar
  content. It is read once and strictly: a schema version other than 1; a missing or unknown field at any level;
  an unknown category, level (A1, A2, B1), `kind` (`gap`, `choose`) or `books` mode (`none`, `patterns`); a duplicate
  category, rule or item id; an id that is not lower-case a–z, digits and hyphens (1–40); an item with fewer than 2 or
  more than 4 options or with a repeated option; an answer index outside the options; a `fixedOrder` that is not a boolean; a `textLang` other than "es" or on a `gap` item; a `feedback` count different
  from the options'; a `gap` item without exactly one "___" or with an `ask`; a `choose` item without `ask` or
  without `text`; a rule with no or more than 4 rule lines, different line counts in English and Spanish, fewer than
  2 or more than 4 examples, a contrast without exactly 2 forms, no or more than 20 aliases (each at most 40 code
  points), fewer than 3 or more than 12 items, or a `patterns` list with none or more than 12 patterns (each 1–5 words
  of a–z, `|` and `*`); more than 200 rules or 20 categories; any text that is blank, over 400 code points (a combining
  mark counts), or that holds a control, format (bidirectional, zero-width), line or paragraph separator or private-use
  character (the same set as the irregular-verb table's texts); a file over 256 KB; or JSON nested deeper than 8
  levels is refused. The tests load the bundled file and compare its SHA-256 with the one below.
- Shape: `schema`, `categories[] {id, title{en,es}}`, `rules[] {id, category, level, title{en,es}, rule{en[],es[]},
  examples[] {en, es}, contrast {forms[2], gloss{en,es}?}, note{en,es}?, spanish{en,es}?, aliases[], books {mode,
  patterns[]?}, practice[] {id, level, kind, text?, ask{en,es}?, options[], answer, feedback[] {en, es}, fixedOrder?, textLang?}}`. The first
  item of a rule is its pretest. Ids are stable (`time-prepositions`, `time-prepositions-1`): saved progress refers to
  them; an id is never reused.
- Option order (shared logic, tested with the vectors below): the options of an item are shown in the order `order` returns unless the item has `fixedOrder: true`; `answer` and `feedback` always refer to the file's order. `seen` is how many times the item has been shown before this time (0 the first time). Seed: the FNV-1a 64-bit hash of the UTF-8 bytes of `<item id>#<seen>` (offset 0xcbf29ce484222325, prime 0x100000001b3, each byte XORed then multiplied, wrapping at 64 bits). Generator: SplitMix64 whose state starts as that seed (state = seed; then, for each value: state += 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15; z = state; z = (z ^ (z >> 30)) × 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9; z = (z ^ (z >> 27)) × 0x94D049BB133111EB; result z ^ (z >> 31); all wrapping at 64 bits, unsigned). Shuffle: `order` = [0 … n−1]; for i from n−1 down to 1: j = next() mod (i+1) (unsigned); swap `order[i]` and `order[j]`. Position k then shows the file's option `order[k]`. Vectors: FNV-1a("a#0") = 0xe649f919048c3037 and its first SplitMix64 output = 0x0851d3f570ff7424; ("his-her-its-1", 1, 3) → [2, 0, 1]; ("his-her-its-1", 2, 3) → [1, 2, 0]; ("question-order-1", 0, 3) → [2, 0, 1]; ("age-and-feelings-1", 5, 3) → [0, 2, 1]; ("do-does-did-1", 0, 3) → [2, 0, 1]; ("x", 0, 2) → [0, 1]. The right answers must not pile up on one position as the learner sees them: over seen 0–9 no position holds more than 40% of the 3-option items (tested).
- Day (shared logic): a day starts at 04:00 in the device's local time zone. The day number of an instant is the number of whole days between 2001-01-01 and the local calendar date of (the instant − 4 hours). Vectors: local 2001-01-02 03:59 → 0; local 2001-01-02 04:00 → 1; local 2001-01-01 02:00 → −1 (never stored).
- Review selection (shared logic, tested with the vectors below), after S7 has built the session's words: W is the number of words, G = min(20 − W, 6) the slots for rule items (0 when W ≥ 20). A candidate is an item of a rule in Review that is due (its due day is not after today) or that has a `tryMiss` flag from an earlier day (a `tryMiss` of today counts as no flag), and that has no `textLang: "es"` unless the translation language or the interface language is Spanish (an excluded item is not counted anywhere below). Rules: each rule with candidates has the key (the oldest day among its candidates, where a flagged candidate counts as the smaller of its `tryMiss` day and its due day, then the rule's position in the file); take them in ascending key order until min(3, ⌊G/2⌋) are taken (one rule with one item when G = 1; none when G = 0). A rule is new when none of its items has `reviewed` (the flag set by the first Review answer to an item; box 0 alone does not say it, because a wrong answer also returns to box 0); at most 1 new rule per session (no day count is stored): a second new rule is skipped, does not count toward the cap, and the next rule in key order takes its place. Items of a rule: its candidates sorted by (flagged first, the oldest flag first; then the oldest due day; then the item's position in the file); take the first 2 (1 if only one is a candidate). "Never the same pair": when those 2 are the rule's `lastPair` (in any order) and a third candidate exists, take the first and the third instead. A visit with 2 items from a rule sets its `lastPair` to them; a visit with fewer than 2 items from it clears it; a rule that only had items waiting keeps it. A flag is cleared when its item is shown. "Passed its last box" (S25) is box 5, the box after S7's last interval. Order of the rule items: first items of each rule in the rules' order, then the second items (r1a, r2a, r3a, r1b, r2b, r3b). Placement, in integer arithmetic: with m rule items, item j (from 0) goes after word number ((j+1) × W + m) div (m+1) (before all words when that is 0: a session without words starts with a rule item, a session with words never does); items with the same number keep their order. Waiting: a candidate that gets no slot (its rule was not taken, or it was the third candidate of a taken rule) is left out; N, the number in the end-screen notice, is the number of candidates left out; `waited` of each left-out item goes up by 1 at most once per review day. Vectors (today is day 6): slots: W = 10 → G = 6; W = 14 → 6; W = 15 → 5; W = 19 → 1; W = 20 → 0; W = 25 → 0. Placement: W = 10, m = 6 → after words 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9; W = 3, m = 2 → 1, 2; W = 1, m = 2 → 1, 1; W = 0, m = 4 → 0, 0, 0, 0. Rules, G = 6: R1 (new, oldest due 8, file position 3), R2 (new, due 9, position 1), R3 (due 10, position 0), R4 (due 11, position 2) → R1, R3, R4 (R2 is a second new rule: skipped). R3 (due 10, position 0) and R2 (due 10, position 5) → R3 then R2 (file position breaks the tie). A rule whose only candidate is flagged `tryMiss` 3 with due 10 has the key 3 and comes before a rule due 5. Items: a (due 5, position 2), b (due 5, position 1), c (due 9, position 3, `tryMiss` 4) → [c, b]; with `lastPair` {c, b} → [c, a]; with c's `tryMiss` 6 (today) and c not due → [b, a]. Spanish filter: with an item d (due 4, position 0, `textLang: "es"`) added, an English-only learner gets [c, b] and N counts a only; with Spanish, [c, d] (the flagged item stays first) and N counts a and b.
- Content rules (tested): every item's `level` is not above its rule's; every rule has at least 6 items, at least 3 of them without `textLang`, and the first (the pretest) never has one; the right answer, as the learner sees it, is spread over the positions (Option order); every `choose` item has a visible stimulus; rules, examples
  and contrasts show only correct English.
- Rule ids in this release (wave 1, 15 rules, 101 items): his-her-its, irregular-past, do-does-did, subject-it-there, the-a-nothing,
  there-then-than, question-order, present-s, adjective-noun, time-prepositions, place-prepositions, single-negative,
  indirect-questions, present-perfect-past, age-and-feelings. A word that only an old book uses (whither, whence) is a note marked archaic, not a rule. Later waves add rules and change the SHA-256 (this file is updated with them).
- Progress (`tips.json`, schema 2, in the app's own folder, plain name; its format is each platform's own and is never copied between platforms, its limits and meaning are shared and tested with the same vectors): per rule id `opened` (a Unix time in whole seconds), attempts and rights (display only), whether it is in Review, `pretestDone`, `pretestSkipped`, `lastPair` (the ids of the 2 items of the rule's last Review visit; kept only when they are 2 different items of that rule), `rightDays` (at most 2 entries `{day, item}`: a right answer to an item in Try it or Review, pretest excluded, is added when the list is empty, or when its day and its item both differ from the entry already there; the rule is "Practiced" with 2 entries), and per item `{box 0–5, due (a day number, or none), seen, missed, tryMiss (a day number, or none), waited, reviewed (a boolean)}`. At most 200 entries (the ones opened longest ago go first), 12 items each, counts 0–1,000 (`waited` too). Read back bounded: a file over 384 KB (the most the other limits allow is about 290 KB, tested; the rules file stays 256 KB), JSON nested more than 8 containers deep, or JSON that cannot be parsed gives empty progress (never a crash); a key outside the schema is dropped; a rule or item id that is not 1–40 characters of a–z, 0–9 and hyphen or is not in the bundled rules is dropped; a day number must be an integer, 0 to today (`due`: to today + 31) and anything else is dropped before any date conversion; `opened` must lie between 2020-01-01 and now, else the rule's entry keeps no time; counts and the box are brought into range with saturating arithmetic (no overflow); rights never above attempts, missed never above seen; `rightDays` keeps at most 2 entries with different days and different items. Another schema gives empty progress. If the bundled rules did not load, progress is not written (the saved file stays as it is). Deleting the app's data removes it. No telemetry: nothing about Grammar leaves the device. Measured on the device only (never sent): per item the box, due day and results, so a later version can compare the first try of a pretest with the first practice and recall after 7 days; a skipped pretest is left out of that. Practiced boundary vectors (each line a new rule; entries are `(day, item)`): [(5, A)] → not Practiced; [(5, A), (5, B)] → not (same day); [(5, A), (6, A)] → not (same item); [(5, A), (6, B)] → Practiced.
- Search (`GrammarSearch`): lower-case with the locale-independent rule (Swift `lowercased()`, Kotlin `lowercase(Locale.ROOT)`), strip accents (NFD, no combining marks), split on every character that is not a letter (Unicode category L); every query word must prefix a word of the rule's title, rule lines, examples or aliases (both languages).
